Yes. Under the prevailing interpretation of international humanitarian law, the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to Judea and Samaria (the West Bank). However, Israel disputes its de jure applicability, making this a longstanding legal disagreement.

The distinction is between the position adopted by the International Court of Justice (ICJ) and the Israeli government's legal position.

1. The international legal position

The Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 governs the protection of civilians during war and occupation.

The principal legal provision is Common Article 2, which provides that the Convention applies to armed conflicts between contracting states and to occupations arising from those conflicts.

The ICJ addressed this specific question in its 2004 Advisory Opinion concerning the construction of Israel's security barrier.

In paragraph 101, the Court concluded that:

Israel and Jordan were parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention when the 1967 war broke out.

Israel occupied territories previously controlled by Jordan.

The Convention therefore applies to the Palestinian territories east of the Green Line, irrespective of the precise legal status of those territories before 1967.

The ICJ reaffirmed this conclusion in paragraph 96 of its July 19, 2024 Advisory Opinion.

2. Israel's legal position

Israel has historically disputed the Convention's de jure applicability to Judea and Samaria.

Its principal arguments include:

No recognized prior sovereign: Jordan's annexation of the West Bank in 1950 was recognized by only a small number of countries. Israel argues that the territory was not the sovereign territory of another High Contracting Party when Israel took control in 1967.

Interpretation of Article 2: Israel maintains that the Convention's occupation provisions were intended to apply to the occupation of territory belonging to another High Contracting Party.

Humanitarian protections: Israel has stated that it applies the humanitarian provisions of the Convention in its administration of the territories, without conceding that the Convention applies de jure.

These arguments are discussed in the ICJ's 2004 proceedings and in the historical record of Israel's position.

3. What did the ICJ decide in 2024 and 2025?

The ICJ reaffirmed its position in two subsequent advisory opinions:

July 19, 2024: The Court expressly confirmed that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies throughout the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including the West Bank and East Jerusalem. 
International Court of Justice

October 22, 2025: The Court again confirmed that the Convention applies in the Occupied Palestinian Territory and governs relevant obligations of Israel as an occupying power.

One important legal qualification: ICJ advisory opinions are not binding judgments in contentious proceedings between two states, although they carry considerable legal authority.

4. Does this settle the question of sovereignty?

No. This is an important distinction.

The applicability of the Fourth Geneva Convention does not, by itself, determine:

Whether Judea and Samaria is sovereign Israeli territory.

Whether the Palestinian people have a right to self-determination.

Whether Jordan ever possessed valid sovereignty over the territory.

The ultimate status of the territory in a negotiated peace agreement.

The ICJ's position is that the Convention applies regardless of the dispute over the territory's prior sovereign status.

The most precise legal formulation: The Fourth Geneva Convention is considered applicable to Judea and Samaria by the ICJ, the ICRC, and the prevailing international legal position. Israel maintains a contrary interpretation of the Convention's formal applicability while undertaking to observe its humanitarian provisions.
