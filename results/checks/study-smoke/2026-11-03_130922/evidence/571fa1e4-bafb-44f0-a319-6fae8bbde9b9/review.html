Question

Does the Fourth Geneva Convention apply to Judea and Samaria?

Recorded answer

Yes. The Fourth Geneva Convention is generally considered applicable to Judea and Samaria (the West Bank) under international humanitarian law. However, there is a longstanding legal dispute between Israel and much of the international community over the Convention's formal applicability and the legal reasoning behind it.

The answer depends on distinguishing between two questions:

Does the Convention apply as a matter of international law? The prevailing international position is yes.

Does Israel accept that it applies de jure (as a matter of law)? Israel has historically disputed its formal applicability, while stating that it observes its humanitarian provisions in practice.

1. The international community's position

The Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 concerns the protection of civilians during war and occupation.

Article 2 provides that the Convention applies to cases of partial or total occupation of the territory of a High Contracting Party, even if the occupation meets no armed resistance.

The prevailing international interpretation is that:

The West Bank was captured by Israel in the 1967 Six-Day War.

Jordan's prior sovereignty over the territory was not universally recognized.

Nevertheless, the absence of universally recognized Jordanian sovereignty does not prevent the Convention from applying.

Israel exercises effective control over the territory, making the law of occupation applicable.

This is the position adopted by the United Nations, the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), and the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

2. Israel's legal position

Israel's traditional legal argument is more specific than simply denying that the territory is occupied.

It rests on several points:

Jordanian sovereignty: Jordan's annexation of the West Bank in 1950 was recognized by only a small number of countries. Israel argues that Jordan was not the legitimate sovereign over the territory in 1967.

Article 2: Israel argues that the Convention's provisions concerning occupation of the territory of another High Contracting Party do not straightforwardly apply because the West Bank was not internationally recognized as Jordanian sovereign territory.

Historical status: Israel argues that the territory's status is unique, given its history as part of the British Mandate and the unresolved question of sovereignty.

Israel has nevertheless historically undertaken to respect the Convention's humanitarian provisions in its administration of the territory.

These arguments are reflected in Israeli legal scholarship and historical statements by Israeli representatives.

3. What did the International Court of Justice decide?

There are two particularly important decisions.

2004 – The Wall Advisory Opinion

In paragraph 101, the ICJ concluded that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to the Palestinian territories occupied by Israel in 1967.

Importantly, the Court expressly stated that it did not need to investigate the precise prior sovereign status of those territories.

This directly addresses one of Israel's principal legal arguments. 
Question of Palestine

2024 – The Advisory Opinion on the Occupied Palestinian Territory

On July 19, 2024, the ICJ reaffirmed the applicability of the Fourth Geneva Convention.

In paragraph 96, it explained that Israel, Jordan and Egypt were all parties to the Convention when the 1967 armed conflict began, and therefore the Convention applies to the Occupied Palestinian Territory.

5. An important legal distinction

The ICJ's advisory opinions are not binding judgments in contentious proceedings between two states. Nevertheless, they carry considerable legal authority and represent the Court's interpretation of international law.

Also, the applicability of the Fourth Geneva Convention does not itself settle the question of ultimate sovereignty over Judea and Samaria. Those are separate legal questions.

Bottom line

There are two ways to answer your question:

Under the prevailing interpretation of international law: Yes, the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to Judea and Samaria.

Under Israel's traditional legal interpretation: Its formal de jure applicability is disputed because of the territory's unique sovereign history, although Israel has undertaken to observe its humanitarian provisions.

The ICJ has expressly rejected the argument that uncertainty over Jordan's sovereignty prevents the Convention from applying.
