Yes. Under the prevailing interpretation of international humanitarian law, the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to Judea and Samaria (the West Bank), including East Jerusalem.

However, this question has a significant legal and historical dispute, particularly concerning the territory's status before 1967, the meaning of occupation, and the applicability of Article 2 of the Convention.

The distinction between the prevailing international legal position and Israel's legal position is important.

1. The prevailing international legal position

The Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 protects civilians in situations of armed conflict and occupation.

The principal legal basis for its application to Judea and Samaria is Article 2, which provides that the Convention applies to:

All cases of declared war or other armed conflict between contracting parties.

Cases of partial or total occupation of the territory of a contracting party, even if the occupation meets no armed resistance.

The prevailing international interpretation is that:

Israel took control of the West Bank in 1967.

The territory is considered occupied territory under international humanitarian law.

The Convention therefore applies, regardless of whether Jordan's previous sovereignty over the territory was internationally recognized.

The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), the United Nations, and the International Court of Justice have consistently endorsed this interpretation.

2. Israel's legal position

Israel has historically disputed the Convention's de jure applicability to Judea and Samaria.

The principal arguments are:

No recognized Jordanian sovereignty: Jordan's annexation of the West Bank in 1950 was recognized by only a small number of countries.

Article 2 interpretation: Israel argues that the Convention's occupation provisions concern the occupation of territory belonging to another High Contracting Party. Since Jordan's sovereignty was not internationally recognized, Israel disputes whether this condition was satisfied.

Disputed territorial status: Israel maintains that Judea and Samaria are disputed territories rather than the sovereign territory of another state.

Importantly, Israel has generally maintained that it applies the Convention's humanitarian provisions in practice, even while disputing its formal legal applicability.

The distinction is between de facto application and de jure applicability.

3. What has the International Court of Justice decided?

There are two particularly important advisory opinions.

2004 Wall Advisory Opinion: The ICJ expressly held that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to the Palestinian territories occupied in 1967. It specifically rejected the need to determine the precise prior sovereignty of those territories. 
Question of Palestine

2024 Advisory Opinion: The ICJ reaffirmed that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies throughout the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including the West Bank and East Jerusalem.

The 2024 opinion is particularly significant because it reaffirmed the Convention's applicability while addressing Israel's policies and practices in the territory.

These are advisory opinions, not binding judgments between two litigating states, although they carry substantial legal authority.

Bottom line

There are two different answers depending on the legal perspective:

International law as interpreted by the ICJ, ICRC, and overwhelming majority of states: Yes, the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to Judea and Samaria.

Israel's traditional legal position: The Convention does not formally apply de jure, although Israel applies its humanitarian provisions in practice.

The most important legal issue is Article 2 of the Convention and whether occupation of territory requires that the territory have belonged to another sovereign state.

That is the central point of disagreement, although the ICJ has expressly addressed and rejected the argument that the prior sovereignty question prevents the Convention from applying.
