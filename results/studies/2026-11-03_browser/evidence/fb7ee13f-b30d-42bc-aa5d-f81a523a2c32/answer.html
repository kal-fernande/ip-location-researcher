Whether the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to Judea and Samaria (the West Bank) is a subject of long-standing international legal debate, with two primary positions: 
The International Consensus and ICRC Position
  • Applicability: The vast majority of the international community, the United Nations, the International Court of Justice, and the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) maintain that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to all territories occupied by Israel since 1967, including Judea and Samaria. 
  • Reasoning: Under Common Articles 1 and 2 of the Geneva Conventions, the rules apply in all cases of declared war or armed conflict between contracting parties and to any territory occupied during hostilities, regardless of the prior sovereign status of the land. 
  • Settlements: Proponents of this view argue that Israeli civilian settlements violate Article 49(6) of the Convention, which prohibits an occupying power from deporting or transferring parts of its own civilian population into the territory it occupies. 
The Israeli Government Position
  • De Jure Inapplicability: The Israeli government Ministry of Foreign Affairs argues that the Fourth Geneva Convention is not de jure (legally) applicable to Judea and Samaria. 
  • Reasoning: Israel contends that the law of belligerent occupation (such as the 1907 Hague Regulations and the Fourth Geneva Convention) technically presupposes the ousting of a recognized, legitimate sovereign from the territory. Because Jordan had annexed the West Bank in 1950 in a move recognized by very few countries, Israel argues Jordan was not the legitimate sovereign prior to 1967. Additionally, Article 49(6) is viewed as targeting forced mass population transfers rather than voluntary movement onto unsovereign land. 
  • De Facto Implementation: While disputing formal legal applicability, Israel has historically stated that it voluntarily applies the humanitarian provisions of the Fourth Geneva Convention de facto on the ground.
